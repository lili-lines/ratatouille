The contact process consists of 2 steps: first, read the whisker, then decide what it means.

## 1. Acquisition, from angle to a number

#### 1.1 AS5600 output
The AS5600 angle sensor can output its value in 3 ways:

| Mode | What the chip outputs | How you read it |
|---|---|---|
| **PWM** | a pulse whose width = the angle | a timer pin |
| **I2C** | a digital number in a register | the I2C bus |
| **Analog** 🐭 | a voltage (0–3.3 $V$) = the angle | an ADC (Analog-to-Digital Converter) pin |

**PWM (Pulse Width Modulation)** = a signal switched on/off very fast. The angle is encoded by the duration of the "on" time, not by the voltage level. It gives the full angle, but to read it you must time a whole pulse, so each reading waits one full cycle → slow read speed and a low sampling rate (few readings/s).

**I2C (Inter-Integrated Circuit)** = the AS5600 has a fixed I2C address (0x36) you cannot change, so two of them (left + right whisker) cannot share one bus: they would clash, or need an extra switching chip (a multiplexer).

🐭 **Analog** = each sensor gets its own wire to its own ADC pin. Two sensors = two pins, no clash. Analog is also faster: the read is instant, so the robot reacts quickly when a whisker hits a wall. And it is easy to clean: being an analog signal, a simple RC filter removes the motor noise. <br>

RC = a Resistor + a Capacitor wired together, it lets the slow useful signal pass and blocks the fast noise.

#### 1.2 Experiment, read the whiskers over USB

**Objectif** <br>
The goal is to read the AS5600 angle on the Teensy, no motors yet, powered over USB.

<aside style="float:right; width:220px; margin:4px -300px 8px 18px; padding:10px 14px; background:#f6e9fb; border:1px solid #a21caf; border-radius:6px; font-size:0.9rem;">
<strong> 🧲 MAGNET, diametric or axial, how to know?</strong><br>
Bring another magnet (or a paperclip) close:
<ul style="margin:6px 0; padding-left:18px;">
<li>sticks mostly on the 2 flat faces → axial ❌</li>
<li>sticks on one side of the round face and repels the opposite side → diametric ✅</li>
</ul>

**Materiel:** <br>
. Teensy 4.0 + USB cable<br>
. 1–2 AS5600 modules + a diametric Ø4 magnet <br>
. 830-point breadboard (MB-102) <br>
. male-to-male Dupont jumper wires<br>
. soldering iron + solder <br>
. software: Arduino IDE


**Montage** <br>
Wiring per whisker ×2, so two ADC pins (14/A0 & 15/A1):

<figure style="margin:0;">
  <img src="{{ '/assets/img/xp_as5600.png' | relative_url }}" style="max-width:100%; display:block; margin:0 auto;">
  <figcaption style="text-align:center; font-size:0.85rem;">Breadboard example for 1 whisker (14/A0)</figcaption>
</figure>

. VCC = the + power pin (here 3.3 V) <br>
. GND = ground, the − pin (0 V, the common reference) <br>
. OUT = the signal output, just speaks (0 to 3.3 V depending on the angle), no powers <br>
. DIR = GND, it sets the counting direction

**Protocole** <br>
1. solder the header pins on the Teensy and on the AS5600 modules <br>
2. wire as in the diagram: VCC → 3.3 V, GND → GND, DIR → GND, OUT → A0 (A1 for the 2nd sensor) <br>
3. upload [xp1_as5600.cpp]({{ '/algo/xp1_as5600.cpp' | relative_url }}), it prints A0 and A1 in a loop <br>
4. open the Serial Monitor <br>
5. turn the magnet by hand in front of the chip and watch the value

`analogRead(A0)` returns 0…4095, the whisker's bending as a number. The sketch sets `analogReadResolution(12)` because the AS5600 is natively 12-bit, without it the Teensy answers on 0…1023.

**Critère de réussite** <br>
. at rest the value does not drift <br>
. a full turn of the magnet sweeps the whole scale, not a fraction of it. If it is truncated, the magnet is too far or off-centre <br>
. back to the same magnet position → back to the same value, approximately <br>

For this first experiment the magnet is turned by hand & the values are read by eye on the Serial Monitor, so everything here is approximate. So the criteria are validated qualitatively. The precise angle & value curve comes later, once the whisker sits on a fixed structure.

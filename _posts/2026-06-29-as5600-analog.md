The contact process consists of 2 steps: first, read the whisker, then decide what it means.

## 1. Acquisition, from angle to a number

#### **AS5600 output**
The AS5600 angle sensor can output its value in 3 ways:

| Mode | What the chip outputs | How you read it |
|---|---|---|
| **PWM** | a pulse whose width = the angle | a timer pin |
| **I2C** | a digital number in a register | the I2C bus |
| **Analog** 🐭 | a voltage (0–3.3 $V$) = the angle | an ADC (Analog-to-Digital Converter) pin |

**PWM (Pulse Width Modulation)** = a signal switched on/off very fast. The angle is encoded by the duration of the "on" time, not by the voltage level. It gives the full angle, but to read it you must time a whole pulse, so each reading waits one full cycle → slow read speed and a low sampling rate (few readings/s).

**I2C (Inter-Integrated Circuit)** = the AS5600 has a fixed I2C address (0x36) you cannot change, so two of them (left + right whisker) cannot share one bus: they would clash, or need an extra switching chip (a multiplexer).

🐭 **Analog** = each sensor gets its own wire to its own ADC pin. Two sensors = two pins, no clash. Analog is also faster: the read is instant, so the robot reacts quickly when a whisker hits a wall. And it is easy to clean: being an analog signal, a simple RC filter removes the motor noise. <br>

RC = a Resistor + a Capacitor wired together, it lets the slow useful signal pass and blocks the fast noise.

#### **Experiment, read the whiskers over USB**

The goal is to read the AS5600 angle on the Teensy, no motors yet, powered over USB.

The setup: Teensy + the two AS5600 modules. USB gives the 3.3 V the sensors need.

Hardware: <br>
. Teensy 4.0 with male header pins soldered (2 rows, 28 pins) <br>
. 1–2 AS5600 modules + a diametric Ø6 magnet <br>
. 830-point breadboard (MB-102) <br>
. male-to-male Dupont jumper wires (4 are enough) <br>
. a USB cable <br>
. soldering iron + solder (to solder the pins) <br>
. software: Arduino IDE + Teensy support

Wiring per whisker ×2, so two ADC pins (A0 and A1):
```
USB PC ── Teensy
           ├─ 3.3 V ──→ AS5600 VDD  (+)
           ├─ GND   ──→ AS5600 GND  (- the ground, black)
           └─ A0/A1 ←── AS5600 OUT  (the signal, voltage = angle)
```
Meaning: <br>
. VDD = the + power pin (here 3.3 V) <br>
. GND = Ground = the − pin (0 V, the common reference) <br>
. OUT = the signal output, it speaks (0 to 3.3 V depending on the angle), it powers nothing <br>

Read: `analogRead(A0)` returns 0…1023, the whisker's bending as a number.

## 2. Interpretation, from a number to a decision

#### **What do we do with the number ?**
Once the angle is a number, we watch for the release: the moment a whisker, bent against a wall, springs back. Two cases:

1. the robot drifts slightly from the wall → the whisker releases slowly
2. the wall ends: intersection → the whisker releases suddenly

#### **Derivative + threshold**
We tell the two apart with the derivative, how fast the signal changes: <br>
. slow release → small derivative <br>
. sudden release → large derivative, a spike <br>

So we put a threshold on the derivative: <br>
. above it → "The wall just ended, there is an opening here" <br>
. below → "I'm just drifting, keep following the wall"

Detecting one specific event by a criterion like this is called discrimination.

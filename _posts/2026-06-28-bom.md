## 1. Brain & power
Listed in the order of the power chain. What comes $IN$ what goes $OUT$, the current it draws & the limit that kills it.

| Component (role) | IN | OUT | current drawn | ⚠️ Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LiPo 2S (battery, 55×30×12 mm) | — | 7.4 $V$ → fuse | source | 7.4 $V$ nominal [6.0, 8.4] |
| Fuse (protection, 5×20 mm) | 7.4 $V$ ← battery | 7.4 $V$ → switch | 0 passive | 2 $A$ slow-blow, 250 $V$ |
| Slide switch (cut-off, 10 mm) | 7.4 $V$ ← fuse | 7.4 $V$ → driver + MP1584 | 0 passive | min ≥ 3 $A$ & ≥ 10 $V$ |
| MP1584 (buck, 22×17 mm) | 7.4 $V$ ← switch | 5 $V$ → Teensy VIN | 0.02 $A$ | pre-set 5 $V$ (fixed version), check with a multimeter before wiring the Teensy |
| Teensy 4.0 (MCU, brain, 35×18 mm) | 5 $V$ ← MP1584 (VIN) | 3.3 $V$ → sensors | 0.1 $A$ (MCU core) | power VIN 3.6–5.5 $V$, logic NOT 5 $V$-tolerant |

*Note: The 3.3 V regulator is built into the Teensy. Logic = signals/information & power = energy. (The 0.1 A is the MCU core only; the sensors it feeds are counted in §3–4, not twice.)*

## 2. Movement
Listed in the order energy flows to the ground. <br>
driver → motors → wheels

| Component (role) | IN | OUT | A drawn | ⚠️ Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TB6612FNG (2 H-bridge driver, 20×15 mm) | VM 7.4 $V$ ← switch, VCC 3.3 $V$ + PWM ← Teensy | chopped 7.4 $V$ → motors ×2 | 0.01 $A$ logic only | 1.2 $A$ continuous & 3.2 $A$ peak per motor, STBY = HIGH (3.3 V) |
| N20 motors ×2 (6 $V$, gear ratio ~50–100:1 (à mesurer 📏), hall encoder) | chopped 7.4 $V$ ← driver (mean ≈ 6 $V$) | torque → wheels, quadrature 3.3 $V$ → Teensy | ~3 $A$ | 6 $V$ → keep the PWM duty ≈ 81 % (7.4 × 0.81 ≈ 6 V)|
| Wheels ×2 (N20 Ø32 mm silicone) | torque ← motors | motion on the floor | 0 passive | Ø32 mm, circumference ≈ 100 mm, sets the distance-per-encoder-tick |
| Rear support (PTFE skid / ball caster) | — | 3rd contact point | 0 passive | — |

*Note: chopped = on/off fast. Hall encoder = the integrated magnetic revolution counter, which provides the distance traveled. Torque=power, quadrature=signal. Ø32 → 1 turn = ~100 mm → sets the mm-per-tick for odometry.*

## 3. Analog whiskers
Listed in the order the information travels. From the wall, through the mechanics, to a number in the Teensy.

| Component (role) | IN | OUT | A drawn | ⚠️ Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low-friction tip ×2 (PTFE sleeve / micro-ball) | wall contact | force → rod | 0 passive | slide without snagging |
| Whisker rod ×2 (carbon fiber Ø0.5 mm) | force ← tip | torque → pivot | 0 passive | too soft = no signal, too stiff = it pushes the robot away |
| Pivot ×2 (MR63 bearing, 3×6×2.5 mm) | torque ← rod | rotation → magnet | 0 passive | bore Ø3 mm |
| Damped return ×2 (micro-spring + rubber) | rotation ← pivot | centred rest position | 0 passive | too strong = the whisker won't bend, too weak = it oscillates |
| Magnet ×2 (diametric Ø6 mm) | rotation ← pivot | magnetic field → AS5600 | 0 passive | must be diametrically magnetized not axially |
| AS5600 ×2 (12-bit magnetic angle sensor, module 24×15 mm) | 3.3 $V$ ← Teensy, field ← magnet | analog 0–3.3 $V$ → Teensy ADC (1 pin each) | 0.03 $A$ | power [3.3 5 $V$], OUT max 3.3 $V$ for ADC |

*Note: wall contact (mechanical) → rotation (pivot + magnet) → magnetic field → voltage (AS5600) → number (Teensy ADC) electric part.*

## 4. Inertial sensing
The mouse 🐭 needs to know two things about itself: how far it has come, and which way it's looking. One sensor for each.

| Component (role) | IN | OUT | A drawn | ⚠️ Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wheel encoders ×2 (hall, built into the N20 motors, resolution CPR) (à mesurer 📏) | 3.3 $V$ ← Teensy, wheel rotation | quadrature pulses 3.3 $V$ → Teensy | 0.02 $A$ | N20 = low resolution, read both channels (A+B) to keep precision, they lie when a wheel slips |
| MPU-6050 / GY-521 (6-axis IMU: gyro + accelerometer, 21×16 mm) | 3.3 $V$ ← Teensy | I2C data 3.3 $V$, address 0x68 → Teensy | 0.004 $A$ | I2C only, no SPI, the gyro drifts slowly over time |

*Note:  The two sensors are complementary: the encoders give distance but drift when a wheel slips, while the gyro gives heading but drifts over time. Fusing them gives a stable position + heading.*

## 5. Clean signal chain
Motor noise has 4 defenders.

| Component (role) | IN | OUT | A drawn | ⚠️ Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reservoir capacitor (≥ 470 µF electrolytic, Ø10×12 mm) | 7.4 $V$ rail, across Voltage Motor (VM) at the driver | absorbs the motor current spikes → stable VM | 0 passive | rated ≥ 16 $V$ (25 $V$ best), polarised: + on VM, − on GND |
| RC filter ×2 (R 1 kΩ + C 100 nF) | noisy analog 0–3.3 $V$ ← AS5600 | clean analog → Teensy ADC | 0 passive | cut-off fc = 1/(2πRC) ≈ 1.6 kHz, above the whisker's useful speed, below the PWM noise |
| Decoupling ×n (100 nF per chip) | 3.3 $V$ rail at each chip | stable local 3.3 $V$ → that chip | 0 passive | sit a few mm of the chip's VDD+/GND- pins |
| Star ground (wiring, not a part) | all GND returns | one single common point | 0 passive | never daisy-chain the motor GND through the sensor GND |

*Note: across = in parallel, between the two pins.* <br>
*if you pick R or C wrong, the cut-off moves:* <br>
*. fc too low → you also cut the whisker signal* <br>
*. fc too high → you let the noise through (the signal shakes again)* <br>
*→ The filter threshold 1 kΩ + 100 nF are chosen on purpose to land at ~1.6 kHz* <br>
*Each chip has its own decoupling capacitor (Teensy, AS5600, MPU-6050...)*

3D-print parts, everything here is mechanical.

- **Chassis**: carries every other part.
- **Motor brackets ×2**: hold the two N20 motors. The two axes must be parallel and at the same height, or the robot curves instead of going straight.
- **Whisker module ×2**: carry the AS5600 + pivot + magnet. Must hold the magnet 0.5–3 mm above the AS5600 centre, on the pivot axis.
- **Screws**: use nylon (nyloc) nuts: plain nuts unscrew themselves with the vibrations.
- **Wiring**: Dupont + perfboard, connects everything. Keep motor wires away from sensor wires + twist them, or the noise couples back in.

**Σ A (6) mechanics = 0 $A$**


## ✅ Coherence check

Three things must hold, and all three do.

1. One power chain, three rails:
7.4 $V$ (LiPo) → fuse → switch → motors (VM), and → MP1584 → 5 $V$ → Teensy → 3.3 $V$ → sensors.

2. Everything logical talks 3.3 $V$, so no level shifter is needed:
Teensy, TB6612FNG logic, AS5600 OUT, MPU-6050 I2C, encoders. The Teensy is not 5 $V$-tolerant, so this is mandatory.

3. The budgets fit:

| Check | Value | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 3.3 $V$ load vs Teensy LDO | 54 $mA$ / 250 $mA$ | ✅ |
| Peak draw vs battery | ~3.2 $A$ / ~8 $A$ | ✅ |
| Fuse + switch vs motor peak | 2 $A$ slow-blow & ≥ 3 $A$ vs ~3 $A$ | ✅ |


## 🔋 Total current draw (Σ all A)

| Section | Σ $A$ |
|---|---|
| 1. Brain & power | 0.12 |
| 2. Movement | 3.01 |
| 3. Analog whiskers | 0.03 |
| 4. Inertial sensing | 0.024 |
| 5. Clean signal chain | 0 |
| 6. Chassis & mechanics | 0 |
| **TOTAL (peak)** | **≈ 3.2 $A$** |

The total is dominated by the motors at peak/stall (~3 $A$) — that's what sizes the battery (C-rating), the slide switch, the 2 A fuse and the wiring. Logic + all sensors together draw < 0.2 $A$.

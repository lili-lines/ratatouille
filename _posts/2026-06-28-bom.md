#### 1.4 Inertial sensing
Ratatouille 🐭 needs to know two things: how far it has come, and which way it's looking. So 1 sensor for each. <br>
**🛈** 2 sensors are complementary, the encoders give distance but drift when a wheel slips, while the gyro gives heading but drifts over time. Fusing them gives a stable position + heading.

<div style="display:flex; gap:16px; align-items:center;" markdown="1">
<div style="flex:1;" markdown="1">
**Wheel encoders** <br>
×2, hall, built into the N20 motors, resolution CPR <br>
IN: 3.3V ← Teensy, wheel rotation <br>
OUT: quadrature pulses 3.3V → Teensy, 0.02A <br>
**🛈** N20 = low resolution, read both channels (A+B) to keep precision, they lie when a wheel slips
</div>
</div>

<div style="display:flex; gap:16px; align-items:center;" markdown="1">
<div style="flex:1;" markdown="1">
**Gyro / IMU** <br>
MPU-6050 / GY-521, 6-axis (gyro + accelerometer), 21×16 mm, ~2g <br>
IN: 3.3V ← Teensy <br>
OUT: I2C data 3.3V, address 0x68 → Teensy, 0.004A <br>
**🛈** I2C only, no SPI, the gyro drifts slowly over time
</div>
<img src="{{ '/assets/img/gyro_MPU-6050.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="MPU-6050 IMU" style="flex:0 0 160px; max-width:160px; border-radius:6px;">
</div>

**Σ $A$ (1.4) inertial = 0.024 $A$**


## 2. Electronic anatomy

#### 2.1 Power

Total current draw Σ $A$ & total weight Σ g

<div style="display:flex; gap:16px; align-items:flex-start;" markdown="1">
<div style="flex:0.9; min-width:0;" markdown="1">

| Section | Σ $A$ | Σ g |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 Brain | 0.12 | ~5g |
| 1.2 Movement | 3.01 | ~40g |
| 1.3 Analog whiskers | 0.03 | ~5g |
| 1.4 Inertial sensing | 0.024 | ~2g |
| 2.1 Power | — | ~52g |
| 2.2 Signal | 0 | ~2g |
| 3. Chassis | 0 | 🚧 |
| **TOTAL (peak)** | **≈3.2** | **≈106g** |

</div>
<div style="flex:1; min-width:0;" markdown="1">
The total ≈ 3.2 $A$, that's what sizes the battery (C-rating), slide switch, 2A(T) fuse & wiring. Logic + all sensors together draw < 0.2 $A$: the motors draw by far the most current. <br>
At 10C the min is 320 mAh (0.32Ah × 10 = 3.2A), to have a margin so we look for 500 mAh: 0.5Ah × 10 = 5A max, above the peak.
The supplier only had 800 mAh, a comfortable margin above the peak, and more runtime. <br>
</div>
</div>

**Power chain** <br>
7.4V (LiPo) → fuse → switch → motors (VM), and → MP1584 → 5V → Teensy → 3.3V → sensors.

<div style="display:flex; gap:16px; align-items:center;" markdown="1">
<div style="flex:1;" markdown="1">
**Battery** <br>
LiPo 2S, 55×30×12mm, 3PIN 7.4V SM 800mAh 10C, ~48g (cf. calculation above) <br>
**⚠** 7.4V nominal [6.0, 8.4] <br>
**🛈** C-rating = how fast the battery empties, e.g. 1C = 1h <br>
Max current (A) = Capacity (Ah) × C-rating ⇔ 0.8Ah × 10 = 8A <br>
So our battery can supply 8A max.
</div>
<img src="{{ '/assets/img/battery.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="LiPo 2S battery" style="flex:0 0 160px; max-width:160px; border-radius:6px;">
</div>

<div style="display:flex; gap:16px; align-items:center;" markdown="1">
<div style="flex:1;" markdown="1">
**Protection** <br>
Fuse 5×20 mm, ⇆ 7.4V, 0A passive, 2A slow-blow, 250V, ~3g with holder <br>
**🛈** with T (Time-lag), transparent with small metallic mass inside + it requires a holder to connect <br>
**⚠** 2A is below the 3.2A peak on purpose, the time-lag absorbs the brief stall peaks
</div>

#### 2.2 Signal
Motor noise has 4 defenders.

<div style="display:flex; gap:16px; align-items:center;" markdown="1">
<div style="flex:1;" markdown="1">
**Reservoir capacitor** <br>
≥ 470 µF electrolytic, Ø10×12 mm, ~2g <br>
7.4V rail, across Voltage Motor (VM) at the driver <br>
absorbs the motor current spikes → stable VM <br>
rated ≥ 16V (25V best), polarised: + on VM, − on GND
</div>
<img src="{{ '/assets/img/cap_470uF.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="Reservoir capacitor 470uF" style="flex:0 0 160px; max-width:100px; border-radius:6px;">
</div>

<div style="display:flex; gap:16px; align-items:center;" markdown="1">
<div style="flex:1;" markdown="1">
**RC filter** <br>
R 1 kΩ + C 100 nF <br>
IN/OUT: noisy analog 0–3.3V ← AS5600, clean analog → Teensy ADC <br>
cut-off frequency (fc) = 1/(2πRC) = 1.6kHz, + the whisker's useful speed, - the PWM noise <br>

**🛈** if you pick R or C wrong, the cut-off moves: <br>
. fc too low → cut the whisker signal <br>
. fc too high → let the noise through <br>

</div>
<img src="{{ '/assets/img/RC_filter.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="RC filter" style="flex:0 0 160px; max-width:160px; border-radius:6px;">
</div>

<div style="display:flex; gap:16px; align-items:center;" markdown="1">
<div style="flex:1;" markdown="1">
**Decoupling** <br>
100 nF per chip, 3.3V rail at each chip <br>
stable local 3.3V → that chip, sits a **few mm** from the chip's VDD+/GND- pins <br>
**🛈** each chip has its own decoupling capacitor
</div>
<img src="{{ '/assets/img/Condensateur_decouplage_100nF.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="Decoupling capacitor 100nF" style="flex:0 0 160px; max-width:160px; border-radius:6px;">
</div>

<div style="display:flex; gap:16px; align-items:center;" markdown="1">
<div style="flex:1;" markdown="1">
**Wiring** <br> 
Star ground, all GND returns one single common point <br>
never daisy-chain the motor GND through the sensor GND
</div>
<img src="{{ '/assets/img/start_ground.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="Star ground wiring" style="flex:0 0 160px; max-width:160px; border-radius:6px;">
</div>

. **Chassis**: carries every other part. <br>
. **Motor brackets ×2**: hold the two N20 motors. The two axes must be parallel and at the same height, or the robot curves instead of going straight. <br>
. **Whisker module ×2**: carry the AS5600 + pivot + magnet. Must hold the magnet 0.5–3 mm above the AS5600 centre, on the pivot axis. <br>
. **Screws**: use nylon (nyloc) nuts: plain nuts unscrew themselves with the vibrations. <br>
. **Wiring**: Dupont + perfboard, connects everything. Keep motor wires away from sensor wires + twist them, or the noise couples back in. <br>


**Σ $A$ (3) chassis = 0 $A$**

*[ADC]: Analog-to-Digital Converter
*[buck]: step-down converter
*[CPR]: Counts Per Revolution (encoder resolution)

*[MCU]: Microcontroller Unit
*[PCB]: Printed Circuit Board
*[PWM]: Pulse Width Modulation
*[RC]: Resistor + Capacitor filter
*[SPI]: Serial Peripheral Interface
*[STBY]: Standby (LOW = asleep, HIGH = awake)
*[VCC]: logic supply voltage
*[VIN]: Voltage In (power input pin)
*[VM]: Voltage Motor (driver motor supply)
*[2S]: 2 cells in series
*[across]: in parallel, between the two pins
*[2A(T)]: The 2A fuse is below that 3.2 A peak on purpose: being slow-blow (T), it lets the brief stall peaks through but cuts on a sustained overload or a short, which is exactly what we want if the robot stays jammed against a wall.
